What good documentation does

Make the correct process easier to repeat.

A useful SOP is not a transcript of what an expert remembers. It is a tested communication tool that gives the next person enough context to complete the work, recognize decision points, recover from common problems, and know when to ask for help.

I combine editorial structure, technical fluency, teaching instincts, production experience, and hands-on workflow testing. The result can include a polished PDF, an editable Word document, annotated screenshots, checklists, source files, and a practical update path.

Working process

The workflow gets tested, not merely described

  1. Observe

    Gather source material and learn what experienced users do, including the quiet knowledge that never made it into the old instructions.

  2. Map

    Identify the sequence, inputs, decisions, access requirements, handoffs, exceptions, and expected result.

  3. Draft

    Build the document around reader actions, with enough explanation to support judgment without burying the procedure.

  4. Test

    Walk through the instructions, verify assets and links, resolve gaps, and distinguish known limits from errors.

  5. Deliver

    Provide the approved format, editable source, supporting assets, and clear ownership for the next revision.
