A serious editorial partnership

Help the reader see the work you have already done.

Graduate research asks a manuscript to carry a complicated argument across many pages, sources, sections, and expectations. Even strong writers can lose sight of the reader after living inside the project for months or years.

I bring editorial leadership, higher education experience, legal training, issue-spotting discipline, and close attention to structure and language. The goal is not to replace your thinking. It is to help your reasoning, evidence, and authorial choices reach the reader with greater clarity.

Editing support

Different manuscripts need different levels of intervention

Scope is established before editing begins, based on the manuscript stage, timeline, institutional guidance, and the kind of feedback that will be most useful.

01

Developmental editing

Review of central argument, chapter purpose, organization, evidence placement, repetition, gaps, transitions, and the manuscript's overall intellectual path.

02

Line editing

Sentence- and paragraph-level work on clarity, syntax, flow, emphasis, transitions, unnecessary complexity, and consistency while preserving author voice.

03

Consistency review

Attention to headings, terminology, citations, references, tables, figures, abbreviations, and internal cross-references based on the agreed project scope.

Working process

A defined scope, a visible edit, and decisions that remain yours

  1. Assess

    Review the manuscript stage, program or committee requirements, timeline, priorities, and a representative sample before confirming scope.

  2. Plan

    Agree on the level of editing, deliverables, sequence, communication points, and what falls outside the engagement.

  3. Edit

    Work in tracked changes and comments, distinguishing direct corrections from questions, patterns, and larger revision decisions.

  4. Return

    Deliver the edited manuscript with an editorial summary that identifies major patterns, priorities, and decisions still requiring author review.

Academic integrity

Editing supports authorship; it does not replace it.

You remain responsible for the research, analysis, claims, evidence, and final decisions in your manuscript. I do not fabricate sources or data, write undisclosed original scholarship, or guarantee committee approval.

Your university, program, committee, and disciplinary rules govern what editorial assistance is permitted. We define the engagement accordingly, and you retain control over every substantive change.
